# Demo script, Computational Track (target 4 minutes)

**Slide 1 (0:00 to 0:20).** My submission has two halves. A router that scores 78.5 against the baseline's 283.5, and an answer to the question routing leaves open: what is the compiled circuit worth once the device has drifted?

**Slide 2 (0:20 to 1:40).** The scorer's rules decide the design. The gate list must be reproduced exactly, in order, so all the freedom is in the placement, in which qubit moves along which path, and in SWAPs that land in idle slots. Placement is simulated annealing on the weighted interaction graph. Routing goes gate by gate; every candidate SWAP must bring the current gate one step closer, which guarantees the minimum SWAP count and kills oscillation, and among those I choose by SABRE lookahead over the next eight gates plus a charge for the layer the SWAP would occupy. Three reverse passes refine the placement, then restarts with the exact scorer in the loop. Every benchmark valid; two route with zero SWAPs, and vqe_layers at depth six is optimal.

**Slide 3 (1:40 to 3:00).** Now the run-time half. Every edge and qubit on the device drifts after the compiler characterized it, four percent wander plus a slow walk. I executed my routed circuits a thousand times. The compiler promised 0.433 on dense_random. Free-running ends at 0.373. An engineer recalibrating every 200 executions gets 0.390 and dips to 0.304 between visits, because the wander is faster than any schedule. A stability contract holding all 43 channels in-band, one parallel acquisition per execution, delivers 0.429 for the whole run, 99 percent of the promise, at 81 microseconds of host time. The morning re-map that everyone does exists because the compiler's cost model goes stale; maintenance keeps it true.

**Slide 4 (3:00 to 4:00).** And it scales. From 111 to 80,387 channels the residual stays flat and host time grows linearly, 118 milliseconds at the top. The alternative that reconstructs the device's state needs a dense model, 103 gigabytes at that size, and that's before the cubic setup. You can't keep engineers on standby at every site, and you can't reconstruct a device with tens of thousands of channels; the linear in-band contract is the only one of the three that scales. Limits stated on the slide: simulated device, stated floors, and one lesson I learned by breaking it. Thank you.
